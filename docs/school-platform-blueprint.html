Section 01

Two money layers, never one ledger

This is the single most important structural decision in the system, and it is easy to get wrong.

Money moves in two unrelated directions. You charge schools a monthly subscription — that is your revenue. Schools charge parents admission, monthly, exam and event fees — that is the school's revenue, which you merely host. A school's fee income is not yours, is not visible to other schools, and must never appear in the same ledger, report, or table as your subscription income.

Plate 01Money flow — the separation
Layer 1 is billed by you and gates access. Layer 2 is bookkeeping you host on the school's behalf. Separate tables, separate reports, separate permissions.

Consequence

A school falling behind on your subscription must never destroy or hide the school's own financial history. Suspension restricts the workspace; the records survive.

Section 02

Tenancy and the access gate

Every school is an isolated tenant. Every row in every table carries a school_id, and every query filters on it — no exceptions. Subscription status is checked at the gate on each session, never cached in the client.

Plate 02Platform layer over tenant workspaces
The gate is the only thing standing between a school and its workspace. It reads billing status; it does not read fee data.

Section 05

Subscription lifecycle

What happens when a school stops paying is a product decision, not a technical one. Cutting access instantly loses you the customer; never cutting it loses you the revenue. A grace period followed by read-only is the standard middle path.

Plate 04Subscription states and transitions
Past due keeps full access with a payment banner. Suspended drops to read-only with all data preserved. Grace period length and retention window are client decisions — see Section 10.

Section 06

Admission and fee concessions

Your brief specifies a per-student concession set at admission: free, partial, or full fee. This is where a student's whole financial profile is established, so it needs care — a concession decided here quietly governs every invoice generated for years afterwards.

Plate 05Admission flow with concession assignment
Record who approved a concession and why. Without that, waivers become unauditable within one academic year.

Section 07

Fee collection and verification

Your brief has parents attaching a deposit screenshot. That is a claim, not a payment — the money sits in a bank account the system cannot see. So a payment has two states that must never be confused: claimed by the parent, and verified by the office. Only verification credits the ledger.

Plate 06Both payment paths into one ledger
Cash at the office skips the pending state because the clerk is already the verifier. Bank deposits cannot skip it.

Design note

Never delete a rejected payment claim. Keep it with its reason and the original screenshot — fee disputes with parents are common, and the audit trail is the only thing that settles them.

Fee heads to support

  • Admission fee — one-time, at enrolment, subject to concession
  • Monthly tuition — recurring, subject to concession
  • Exam or test fee — per assessment cycle
  • Event fee — ad hoc, collected by teachers per your brief
  • Other charges — transport, uniform, fines; keep this extensible rather than hard-coded

Section 08

The daily academic loop

This is the rhythm that makes the system used every day rather than once a term. Everything a teacher does in the morning reaches a parent the same day.

Plate 07One school day, end to end
Solid arrows are actions a person takes; dashed arrows are what the system pushes out. Step 6 is where daily, weekly and monthly tests aggregate into the quarterly result — the weighting rule is still undecided, see Section 10.

Section 09

Data model

Split into three views for legibility. The governing rule across all of them: every table carries school_id, and tenant isolation is enforced at the query layer, not left to application code to remember.

Plate 08Tenancy and people
STUDENT_DOCUMENT covers B-form, parent ID card, previous school certificate and profile photo as typed rows rather than fixed columns, so a new document type needs no migration.
Plate 09Fees, payments and school expenses
PAYMENT_CLAIM carries the pending, verified and rejected states from Plate 06. PAYMENT_PROOF holds the parent's screenshot. Neither is ever hard-deleted.
Plate 10Academic records
ENROLMENT is the hub: it is the student-in-a-class-in-a-year, and almost every academic and financial record hangs off it rather than off the student directly. That is what makes year rollover tractable — see Section 10.

Section 10

Decisions needed from the client

These are genuine gaps in the brief. Each one changes the schema, so they are far cheaper to settle now than after build.

  1. Blocking

    Academic year rollover

    The brief has no promotion mechanism. What happens each year — are students promoted in bulk, do results gate promotion, and does a repeating student keep the same record? This shapes the enrolment table more than any other single answer.

  2. Blocking

    Result weighting

    Daily, weekly and monthly tests exist, and so do quarterly and annual results. How do the former roll into the latter — fixed weights, best-of-N, teacher discretion? Undefined aggregation means results cannot be computed at all.

  3. Blocking

    Concession scope

    Does a partial or full waiver apply to admission fee only, or to monthly tuition, exam and event fees too? Each is a different billing rule and the brief does not say.

  4. Important

    Multi-campus schools

    If a school group has three branches, is that one tenant with three campuses, or three tenants on three subscriptions? This decides your pricing model as much as your schema.

  5. Important

    Grace and retention windows

    How many days past due before a school goes read-only, and how long is data kept after termination? Both need explicit numbers before Plate 04 can be implemented.

  6. Important

    Attendance granularity

    Once per day, or per period? Per-period is considerably more work for teachers and considerably more useful for secondary schools.

  7. Worth asking

    Sibling discounts

    Common in the region and absent from the brief. If wanted, concessions attach to a family rather than a student — a different relationship entirely.

  8. Worth asking

    Notification channel

    Absence alerts and announcements are only useful if they arrive. In-app only, or SMS and WhatsApp? SMS carries a real per-message cost that has to sit somewhere in your pricing.

  9. Worth asking

    Interface language

    Urdu, English, or both? Retrofitting localisation is far more expensive than building for it from the first screen.
