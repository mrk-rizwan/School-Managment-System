ASMS · Feature Concept

Announcements

How the school reaches parents, students and teachers — one message, the right audience, on every device.

Prepared for client review · Draft concept · 9 slides

Slide 01 — The idea

One place to say something, to exactly the right people

Today a school announces things by WhatsApp group, a note in the diary, or a paper on the notice board. Nobody knows who actually saw it.

Announcements gives the school one screen to write a message, choose who receives it, and see whether it was read. The message arrives in the parent's app, the student's app, or the teacher's app — without anyone being added to a group chat.

Written once

The Principal writes the message a single time, in one place.

Targeted

Everyone, one class, one section, or one family — chosen when sending.

Tracked

The school can see how many recipients have opened it.

Kept

Every announcement stays on record, searchable, tied to the academic year.

Slide 02 — Who can announce

Not everyone should be able to message the whole school

A teacher reminding their own class about homework is routine. That same teacher messaging every parent in the school is not. Sending rights are scoped by role.

SenderWhole schoolOwn class / sectionIndividual family
PrincipalYesYesYes
Office staffIf grantedYesYes
TeacherNoOwn classes onlyOwn students only
ParentNoNoReceives only
StudentNoNoReceives only

The Principal can grant whole-school rights to a specific staff member — an office administrator who handles fee reminders, for example — without making them a principal.

Slide 03 — What a message contains

The announcement itself

Deliberately small. A long form means nobody sends anything.

TitleShort headline the parent sees in the list
MessageThe full text
AudienceWho receives it
PriorityNormal or Urgent
AttachmentA photo, notice or PDF
Send timeNow, or scheduled for later
Expires onDrops off the list after this date
CategoryHoliday, exam, fee, event, general

Fields with a teal border are required. Everything else is optional, so a quick notice takes about fifteen seconds to send.

Slide 04 — Choosing the audience

Audience is built from the school structure, not typed by hand

The sender picks from what already exists in the system — classes, sections, and the families enrolled in them. Nobody maintains a contact list, and nobody is missed when a student joins mid-year.

Everyone→ By role→ By class→ By section→ One family

Each step narrows the group. "All parents of Class 9" is two clicks. "Parents of Class 9-B only" is three. A single family is a search.

One rule to agreeA parent with three children in different classes must receive a school-wide notice once, not three times. The system sends per person, not per student.

Slide 05 — What the parent sees

In the parent's app

Announcements
Urgent · Today School closed tomorrow Due to heavy rain the school will remain closed on Tuesday…
Exam · Yesterday Quarterly exams begin 12 March The datesheet is attached. Please ensure…
Fee · 3 days ago March fee due on the 10th Kindly deposit before the due date to avoid…
Holiday · 1 week ago 14 August — Independence Day The school will be closed. Flag ceremony…

What the parent gets

  • A single list, newest first, with unread messages shown in full colour and read ones faded.
  • Urgent messages carry a red edge and trigger a phone notification. Normal ones simply appear in the list.
  • Each message is tagged by category, so a parent looking for the fee notice can find it a week later.
  • Attachments — a datesheet, a fee voucher, a photo of a notice — open directly in the app.
  • If the parent has more than one child, messages for each child are labelled, but school-wide notices appear only once.

Slide 06 — The important part

Announcements is not one page. It is the school's voice.

Most messages a school sends are not typed by hand. They are produced by something else that happened.

When the Principal adds a public holiday, that is an announcement. When a Parent–Teacher Meeting is scheduled, that is an announcement. When quarterly results are published, or a fee falls due, or a teacher marks a child absent — each one has a message attached to it.

Holidays Events & PTM Results Fees Attendance → Announcements → Parent · Student · Teacher

Why this matters for costIf we build Announcements as a shared service now, every future module gets messaging for free. If we build it as a single page, we will rebuild the same notification code inside Holidays, Events, Results and Fees — four times over.

Slide 07 — How it is delivered

Getting the message to actually arrive

An announcement inside the app only reaches a parent when they open the app. For a fee reminder that is fine. For "school is closed tomorrow" it is not.

ChannelReaches the parentCost to youBest for
In-appWhen they next open the appFreeFee notices, diary, results
Push notificationImmediately, if the app is installedFreeMost announcements
SMSAlways, no app neededPer messageUrgent only — closures, emergencies
WhatsAppAlways, very high open ratePer messageUrgent and fee reminders

Our recommendation: in-app plus push for everything, and SMS or WhatsApp reserved for messages marked Urgent. That keeps the running cost near zero while making sure a genuine emergency actually lands.

Slide 08 — What we need from you

Four decisions before we build

  1. Needed first

    Do parents get SMS or WhatsApp, or app only?

    This is the one decision with an ongoing cost. It also decides whether a parent without a smartphone can use the system at all.

  2. Needed first

    English, Urdu, or both?

    Supporting both from the start is straightforward. Adding Urdu after launch means revisiting every screen.

  3. Important

    Should parents be able to reply?

    One-way announcements are simple. Two-way turns this into a messaging system, with everything that implies — who answers, and how quickly.

  4. Worth confirming

    Does the school need read receipts?

    Knowing that 43 of 60 parents opened the fee notice is useful. Some schools want it; some find it creates pressure to chase people.
