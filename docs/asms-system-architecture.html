Section 03

The mobile client

One React Native codebase. The role on the session decides what the shell renders.

There is no principal app, teacher app or parent app as separate builds. There is one app that fetches a capability set at login and composes its navigation from it. A person who is both a teacher and a coordinator sees both, from one install.

The rule that keeps it small

If a task needs a keyboard, it belongs on the web. Admissions, salary structures, fee head configuration, report card layout and full reports are web-only. The app carries what someone does while standing up.

The principal's inbox

The principal's home screen is a queue of things awaiting a decision, not a dashboard of figures. Each row is one pending-work query, and each opens straight into the action.

Offline behaviour

Three tiers, and only one of them is genuinely hard.

  • Read, cached. Last-known state renders instantly with no network. Attendance lists, diary, class rosters and inbox counts all come from the local store first, then refresh. A parent on a dying connection still sees yesterday's diary.
  • Write, queued. Expense capture and attendance marking hold locally and sync when the connection returns, each carrying a client-generated idempotency key so a retry cannot double-post. Principals record expenses in storerooms; teachers mark registers in corridors.
  • Write, online only. Anything that moves money or grants authority — verifying a payment, confirming a handover, approving a result. These need a server-side check against current state, so they fail closed with a clear message rather than queueing.

Data cost is a design constraint, not an optimisation

A day's text sync is roughly 10–50 KB. One uncompressed student photo is 2–4 MB — more than a month of everything else. Images load on tap, thumbnails are generated server-side at around 20 KB, and nothing prefetches media. The APK itself, at 15–25 MB, costs a parent more data than six months of use, so installation happens over school WiFi at admission or a PTM.

Section 04

Notification delivery

Everything that leaves the system to reach a person goes through one interface.

This is the layer that makes WAHA a safe choice. No module ever calls WAHA directly — they call NotificationService, which decides channels from message priority and what the recipient can actually receive. If a WhatsApp number is banned or a session dies, you change a driver and one config value. Nothing else moves.

Plate 03One interface, four drivers
The delivery log records sent, delivered and failed per channel — it is not a read log. Read status exists only in the app, and on WhatsApp when the recipient has receipts enabled; SMS reports delivery only. Never present delivered as read. The log is also what tells you a WAHA session has quietly died, before a school does.

Two rules still to specify: which message types may reach SMS at all — applied literally, a daily diary would send one SMS per subject per day — and how the service knows what a given guardian can receive. That capability field does not exist yet on the guardian record, and every routing rule above depends on it.

Section 05

Charge campaigns

One mechanism covers monthly fees, exam fees, fines, trips and event collections.

An admin creates a charge, targets an audience with the same picker announcements use, and the system generates one line per student. If a teacher is assigned to collect, it appears in their app with their class roster.

The part that must not be skipped

When a teacher collects cash, the money is in their hands, not the school's. Marking a student paid is a claim; the office confirming the handover is verification. Only verification credits the ledger. Without this split, the books show money the school does not yet have, and a shortfall is invisible.

Plate 04Charge lifecycle, from creation to ledger
Both paths converge on one ledger. Cash handed to the office is verified on the spot; a teacher's collection and a parent's screenshot both wait for confirmation.

Still open

The canonical register lives in CLAUDE.md. This list mirrors the items that bear directly on the architecture; where the two disagree, CLAUDE.md wins.

  • Blocking  Attendance granularity — daily or per period. A period-level table rolls up to daily; a daily table can never be split without a migration.
  • Blocking  Account model — who creates a guardian login, whether one covers several children, phone or email as identifier, and whether students log in at all. Phase 1 starts here.
  • Blocking  Permission model — fixed roles, or roles plus grantable capabilities. Plate 02 shows the recommended shape (roles plus grantable capabilities); it is provisional until the client confirms decision 2.
  • Blocking  Multi-campus — one tenant with campuses, or one tenant per branch. Decides whether school_id is the tenant key.
  • Blocking  Guardian contact capability — how the system knows a parent has a keypad phone, a social bundle, or full data. Plate 03 dereferences this field on every send.
  • Closed  English only. Confirmed 2026-10-01; no Urdu interface and no right-to-left layout.
  • Assumed  Class-test marks reach parents immediately; term and annual results wait for principal approval. Applied to resolve a contradiction in the design document.
  • Blocking  Do concession students pay exam fees and fines? Charge campaigns must decide this at targeting time.
  • Deferrable  Result weighting (open decision 14; deferrable only if ASSESSMENT_WEIGHT is its own table) — how daily, weekly and monthly tests roll into quarterly and annual.
  • Deferrable  Promotion rules (open decision 15; needed before the first year-end) at year rollover: repeats, and whether arrears block promotion.
  • Decide  Partial payment on a charge line — allowed, or all-or-nothing?
  • Decide  One WhatsApp number per school, or one for the platform? Per school is what schools expect and multiplies the sessions you monitor.
  • Decide  Cash basis or accrual for the profit report.
